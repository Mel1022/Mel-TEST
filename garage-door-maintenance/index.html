Most Garage Door Failures Don't Happen Without Warning.

Worn rollers, loose hardware, and drifting opener settings often show early signs — a new noise, a slight hesitation, a door that looks a little uneven — before a full breakdown.

A maintenance visit checks the parts that wear with regular use, so small issues can be addressed before they turn into an inconvenient repair.

Service Overview

Routine maintenance catches small issues before they become bigger repairs — and keeps your door running smoothly.

Every visit starts with a diagnosis, followed by a clear explanation of what we found and the recommended fix — before any work begins.

Common Symptoms

  • Door hasn't been inspected in some time
  • New or increasing noise during operation
  • Door feels slightly slower or less smooth than before
  • Visible wear on rollers, hinges, or weatherstripping
  • Preparing for a season of heavy use

What the Technician Checks

Springs and cable condition

We check spring cycle life remaining, look for coil separation or rust, and inspect cables for fraying or uneven tension on both sides.

Rollers, hinges, and tracks

Worn nylon rollers, cracked hinges, and tracks that are out of plumb are early indicators of bigger problems. We assess all three together since they affect each other.

Lubrication of moving parts

Springs, rollers, and hinges require the right lubricant — not general-purpose spray. We apply proper lubricant to the correct components to reduce wear and noise.

Door balance

A balanced door should hold its position mid-travel without the opener. We test this and adjust spring tension if the door is pulling heavier on one side.

Opener settings and safety sensors

Over time, opener force and limit settings can drift. We verify auto-reverse sensitivity, travel limits, and photo-eye sensor alignment during each maintenance visit.

Weatherstripping and seals

Bottom seals and side seals protect the garage from drafts, water, and pests. We inspect them for cracking or compression loss and note anything that should be replaced.

Why Professional Service Matters

Routine maintenance can catch wear before it leads to a failure.
A well-maintained door tends to run quieter and operate more smoothly.
Small adjustments now can help avoid a more disruptive repair later.

Garage Door Maintenance FAQ

How often should a garage door be maintained?

It depends on usage and the age of the door and opener. A technician can recommend a schedule based on your specific system after an initial inspection.

What's included in a maintenance visit?

A maintenance visit generally includes a check of springs, cables, rollers, tracks, hardware, door balance, opener settings, and safety sensors, along with lubrication of moving parts.
